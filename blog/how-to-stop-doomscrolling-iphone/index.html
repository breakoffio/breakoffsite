How to Stop Doomscrolling on iPhone: A 6pm Plan

November 5, 2026 · 5 min read

To stop doomscrolling on iPhone, do the work before the urge shows up. Pick the one or two apps you scroll at night, block them on a schedule that starts around 6pm, and make that block hard to cancel. Willpower at 11pm loses. A plan set at 6pm does not need willpower.

Why does doomscrolling feel impossible to stop?

Because you are trying to stop it at the worst possible moment. By 11pm you are tired, the day is done, and the feed is one tap away. Decision-making is at its weakest right when the temptation is strongest.

You are not alone in this. The Pew Research Center surveyed 9,750 US adults in 2026, and 53% said they spend too much time on their smartphone. We broke down the rest of that survey in our Pew write-up, including that 41% say their phone hurts their sleep.

Here is our hot take: doomscrolling is an 11pm problem, so the fix has to be set at 6pm. Every plan that starts with "I will just put it down tonight" is a plan made by the person who is about to lose.

What is the plan that survives a bad night?

The goal is simple. Make the scroll harder than the alternative before you are tired. Here is the order we would do it in.

  1. Name the one or two apps that do the damage. Not your whole phone. Whichever feed you open without deciding to. Total hours is a blurry target, which is why we think counting pickups beats counting hours.
  2. Set the block in daylight. Schedule it to start around 6pm and run until morning. The decision is made by the version of you who still has energy.
  3. Make it hard to undo. A block you can dismiss with one tap is a suggestion. We cover what actually holds in how to lock yourself out of Instagram on iPhone, and the same logic works for any feed.
  4. Give your hands something else. Put the charger outside the bedroom, or keep a book on the couch. A blocked app with nothing to replace it just sends you hunting for the next one.
  5. Add a person. A block someone else shares is harder to quietly cancel. More on that below.

Do free controls work for this?

Sometimes. Fox News CyberGuy suggested in a September 24, 2026 piece that people try built-in free controls before spending $59 on a Brick. That is fair advice, and we are not going to argue with starting free.

The catch is the same one we keep running into. A limit you set alone is one tap from gone. If you can undo it at midnight in under a minute, it will eventually lose. We dug into the evidence in does screen time work, and the pattern is consistent: awareness helps, friction helps more.

What if I still reach for my phone at 11pm?

You will, at least at first. That is normal, and it is the point of the block. The reflex fires, the app does not open, and the reflex fades a little each night. Do not treat a slip as proof the plan failed. Treat it as a sign to widen the block by an hour, or to add the second app you forgot about.

The takeaway

You do not need more discipline at 11pm. You need a decision made at 6pm that you cannot easily reverse. Pick two apps, schedule the block, and let tonight's tired version of you off the hook.
