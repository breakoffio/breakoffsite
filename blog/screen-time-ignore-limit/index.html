Screen Time Ignore Limit: How to Turn It Off for Good

November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

To stop Ignore Limit on iPhone, set a Screen Time passcode, then open each App Limit and turn on Block at End of Limit. The Ignore Limit button goes away, you get one more minute, and after that the app asks for the passcode. That works on iOS 16 to 26. iOS 27 is murkier, and we cover it below.

That is the whole fix in one paragraph. The catch is in who knows the passcode, and that is the part most guides skip.

What does Ignore Limit actually do?

Without a passcode, hitting an App Limit on iOS 16 to 26 gives you three choices, according to Detox's November 2026 guide: One More Minute, Remind Me in 15 Minutes, or Ignore Limit for Today, which lasts until midnight.

So the limit is really a speed bump with a skip button. Your thumb learns where the button is within a week. After that, the limit is a pop-up you dismiss on the way into Instagram.

How do you remove Ignore Limit on iOS 16 to 26?

Both Detox and Tech Lockdown describe the same steps:

  1. Set a Screen Time passcode. Go to Settings, Screen Time, Lock Screen Time Settings. Detox suggests a 4 digit code that is different from your phone passcode.
  2. Open App Limits. Settings, Screen Time, App Limits, then tap the limit you want to enforce.
  3. Turn on Block at End of Limit. The toggle only shows up once a passcode is set. Do this for every limit, one at a time.
  4. Check it worked. At the limit, Ignore Limit is replaced by Ask For More Time. You get One More Minute once, then you need the passcode.

Detox also suggests closing the usual workarounds: set app deletions to Don't Allow and keep the date and time on automatic.

What changes in iOS 27?

Apple's own iOS 27 Screen Time guide describes Time Allowances, a daily time budget across app categories. It does not mention App Limits, Block at End of Limit or Ignore Limit at all.

Detox says early hands-on reports show Ignore Limit still appearing for limits you set yourself on iOS 27, and that Apple does not document this. So we are not going to guess at menu names. If you are on iOS 27, look under Settings, Screen Time and see what your phone actually offers. Why does Block at End of Limit turn itself off?

Tech Lockdown flags a quiet gotcha: you may need to re-check that Block at End of Limit is still on every time you change the Screen Time passcode or edit an App Limit, including its schedule. Tweak a limit on Sunday and you might be back to Ignore Limit on Monday without noticing.

Our habit: after any change, open each limit again and look at the toggle. It takes ten seconds.

Is a passcode you set yourself enough?

Here is our honest take. If you set the passcode yourself, Block at End of Limit just turns a one-tap excuse into a four-digit one. At 11pm, four digits is nothing. You know them. Your thumbs know them.

The fix that actually changes behavior is moving the decision to another person. Detox says the same thing: have someone you trust set or hold the code. That is also what the research keeps pointing at. Tools that add real friction beat reminders you can wave off, which we covered in Do Screen Time Apps Work? Blockers Beat Reminders.

The hard part for adults is that nobody wants to text their partner for a code to open Reddit. Which is kind of the point, but it also means people quietly stop using it.

How do parents remove Ignore Limit on a kid's iPhone?

Tech Lockdown's Family Sharing version: go to Family, your child's name, Screen Time, make sure a passcode exists, open App Limits, and turn on Block at End of Limit for each one. You can do it from your own phone or your kid's. On iOS 27, Apple says the parent sets the passcode on their own device. We have more on this in our app blocker for kids guide.
