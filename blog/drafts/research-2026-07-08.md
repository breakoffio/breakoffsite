- The framing of digital wellness is shifting from moralizing to "design literacy." People are learning to spot triggers, quick actions, and variable rewards as engineering choices, not personal failings. Screen-time app downloads are up about 30% on the back of ordinary habits like family downtime and killing red badges.
- More evidence on the body side: a systematic review of university students found an inverse relationship between physical activity and smartphone addiction, and structured exercise programs reduced addiction symptoms over time. Heavy users (5+ hours a day) show about 15% slower reaction times, and 70% of self-described addicts report "digital amnesia," forgetting what they picked the phone up to do.
- Baseline numbers keep climbing: US average is now 4h49m of phone time a day, up from 2h54m in 2022. 57% of Americans say they feel addicted.

## Rank #3: The cheapest screen time intervention is not an app

**Why it ranks third:** Solid evergreen SEO ("how to reduce screen time naturally", "exercise and phone addiction") and a fresh systematic review to anchor it. Less timely than the other two, and we need to be careful not to argue ourselves out of our own product, which is exactly why the honest framing works.

**Suggested title:** Exercise vs App Blockers: What the Research Says About Cutting Screen Time

**Key points to cover:**
- The systematic review: physical activity is inversely related to smartphone addiction in university students, and structured exercise reduced addiction symptoms over time.
- Why it works: the phone fills unstructured time and dopamine gaps. Movement fills both, and it repairs the sleep and attention damage (15% slower reaction times in heavy users, 23% attention gains after even a 48-hour reset).
- Honest conclusion: blockers handle the moment of reach, exercise lowers the urge to reach at all. Use both. We build one of them and we will still tell you to take the walk.

**Sources:**
- https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12304691/
- https://www.demandsage.com/smartphone-addiction-statistics/

## Hot take angle (for rank #1)

Nobody doomscrolls at midnight because they love their phone. They do it because it is the first minute of the day that belongs to them. That is not an attention problem, it is a schedule problem wearing an attention problem's clothes. So no, we are not going to tell you to "just put the phone in another room." We are going to tell you the truth: you are owed that hour. Take it back on purpose, with a book, a shower, actual rest, instead of letting an algorithm collect it as rent. The scroll is not stealing your sleep. It is fencing stolen goods. Your calendar did the stealing.
